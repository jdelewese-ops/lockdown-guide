Identity Lockdown Checklist

A credit freeze at the three big bureaus is a good start, but it doesn't cover everything. Thieves can open bank accounts, phone and utility service, or file a tax return in your name through other doors. These eight steps close the main ones. Each is free and takes about 10–20 minutes.

Before you start

Type each website address yourself or use the buttons below. Never use a link or phone number from a text, email or caller. When a site gives you a PIN or password for your freeze, write it down on paper and keep it with your important papers.

  1. Claim your my Social Security account

    Only one online account is allowed per Social Security number. Opening yours first keeps a thief from opening one and redirecting your benefits.

    • Create the account at ssa.gov and verify your identity.
    • Review your earnings record. Pay you never earned can be a sign someone is working under your number.
    Go to ssa.gov/myaccount

  2. Freeze your credit at all three bureaus

    Stops new credit cards and loans in your name. Freezes are free by federal law, and they don't affect your current cards or your credit score.

    • Do all three. A freeze at one bureau doesn't carry over to the others.
    • If you apply for new credit later, lift the freeze for a few days, then it goes back on.
    Equifax Experian TransUnion

  3. Freeze your ChexSystems report

    Banks check ChexSystems when someone opens a checking or savings account. A credit freeze doesn't cover this. The freeze is free.

    • Create a ChexSystems account, then choose "Security Freeze." You'll need your driver's license or state ID number.
    • Keep the PIN they send. Lift the freeze before you open a new bank account yourself.
    ChexSystems freeze

  4. Freeze your NCTUE report

    Phone, cable, home security and utility companies check NCTUE before opening service. A freeze stops someone setting up service in your name. It's free.

    • Freeze online, or call 866-349-5355.
    • Save the confirmation page online. It has your PIN. A lift can take up to three business days.
    NCTUE freeze

  5. Freeze your LexisNexis report

    Many lenders and insurers use LexisNexis to check identity and risk. This freeze is separate from the big three bureaus, and it's free.

    • Use the online form, or call 1-800-456-1244.
    • They confirm by mail once the freeze is in place.
    LexisNexis freeze

  6. Get an IRS Identity Protection PIN

    A 6-digit PIN that must be on any federal tax return filed with your Social Security number. Without it, a fake return is rejected.

    • Fastest: request it in your IRS online account. If you can't do that, the IRS also offers a mailed form or an in-person appointment.
    • You get a new PIN every year. Give it only to whoever files your taxes.
    • The IRS will never call, text or email asking for it.
    IRS IP PIN page

  7. Turn on bank and card alerts

    Freezes stop new accounts. Alerts catch problems on the accounts you already have, often the same day.

    • In each bank and card app, turn on alerts for withdrawals, transfers, new payees and changes to your contact info.
    • Set a low dollar amount for purchase alerts so small test charges show up.
    • If you're not comfortable with the app, ask your bank branch to set them up with you.

  8. Keep your PINs in one safe place

    You'll need each PIN to lift a freeze later. Losing them means slow replacement by mail.

    • Write each one on paper: which company, the PIN, and the date.
    • Keep it with your will or other important papers, and tell one trusted family member where it is.

    Don't keep these in an email or text to yourself.

Got a call, text or email that seems off?

Don't reply, click or call back the number it gives. Then use one of these:

  • Check it with your bank: call the number on the back of your card.
  • Talk to a person for free: AARP Fraud Watch Helpline, 877-908-3360, weekdays 8 a.m.–8 p.m. Eastern. Open to anyone, member or not.
  • Report it: reportfraud.ftc.gov. If you lost money, also report it at ic3.gov.
